An expense calculation you can understand next week

Updated October 11, 2026

A total answers “how much?” Notes help answer a different question later: “what did I include?”

Use this small shopping list to organize prices, quantities, and delivery before adding them up. All example amounts use the same currency; replace them with your own figures.

Keep each expense separate

ItemCalculationAmount
Storage boxes48 × 3144
Labels25 × 250
Delivery1212
Total144 + 50 + 12206

Label the numbers and keep the steps

  1. Create a calculation page in OmniCalc and name it for the shopping list or project.
  2. Enter 48 × 3. Add “box price” to 48 and “quantity” to 3. Give the whole expression a note such as “storage boxes.”
  3. Calculate the labels and delivery separately, keeping one result for each expense.
  4. Check that the current page contains only the costs you want to add, then use the page total.
  5. Save the page. Search its title or notes in history, and reopen it when a price or quantity changes.

Do not count a subtotal twice

This list adds 144, 50, and 12. If you also include the total of 206 as another expense in the same page total, you will count those costs twice. Keep income and profit calculations separate from a list of expenses.

Check what each line means, check the price and quantity, then check for duplicate amounts.

Why use notes if you already have history?

History keeps the calculation. Notes keep its meaning. A saved expression such as 48 × 3 could describe shopping, work hours, or something else. Labels make it clear what should change when you reuse it.

Use the same approach for other costs

Replace the shopping items with renovation materials, travel expenses, or project costs. Keep related expenses together and give different projects their own pages. Keep different currencies separate instead of adding their amounts directly.
